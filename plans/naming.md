# Naming research

Status: research done, **the owner picks**. Researched October 2026 for
[phase-3.md](./phase-3.md) §1 and §3. Until a name is chosen, everything reads
from `lib/brand.ts` (placeholder "Ćwiczenia").

**Short version.** The name barely moves Google rankings. What learners type in
("polish cases", "polish grammar exercises") is won by the **page titles,
headings and content** of the topic pages, and those carry the keywords
whatever the brand is. So pick a name that is easy to say, spell and remember,
that nobody else in the niche uses, and that reads well next to the words
"Polish grammar" in the App Store title. The top pick is **Polski Gym**. The
runner-up is **Odmiana**.

## 1. What learners search for

### The searches that matter

Without volume data, the list below comes from how competitors title their
pages, which tools and forums keep turning up, and how learners phrase their
questions. That is direction, not numbers.

| Search | Intent | What it tells us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| polish cases / polish cases explained / polish cases chart | Learn what the seven cases are, see a table | The head term of the niche. Many competitors build a page around exactly this phrase (see §2). It is the most valuable page to win. |
| polish genitive case, polish instrumental case, … (one per case) | One case in depth | Forum threads and guides go case by case ([Polish Forums](https://polishforums.com/language/having-hard-time-cases-14225/), [Preply](https://preply.com/en/blog/polish-cases/)). A good candidate for sub-pages later. |
| polish grammar exercises / polish cases exercises / polish declension practice | **Practise**, not read | This is exactly what the app does. Mostly answered today by static tests ([ProPolski](https://testy.propolski.com/en/)) and blog articles that are not interactive ([Vaia](https://www.vaia.com/en-us/explanations/polish/polish-grammar/polish-grammar-exercises)). |
| polish verb conjugation (+ "practice") | Look up or drill verb forms | Answered by conjugator tools ([Logos](https://freelanguage.org/node/87302)) and drills ([ProPolski verbs](https://testy.propolski.com/en/grammar/verbs/), [Polish Verb Blitz](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1378101809)). |
| polish numbers / polish numerals declension | Numbers and the noun after them (`dwa koty` / `pięć kotów`) | Few dedicated practice pages ([Talkpal](https://talkpal.ai/?p=55378) has a static list). A gap the numbers drill fills. |
| learn polish grammar / best app to learn polish | Choosing a course or app | Dominated by big brands and "best apps" lists ([Clozemaster](https://www.clozemaster.com/blog/?p=7221), [Lingopie](https://lingopie.com/blog/apps-to-learn-polish/), [Babbel](https://uk.babbel.com/polish-grammar)). Hard to rank for; better to get *listed* in those articles. |

### Size of the audience (public figures, not search volumes)

- Duolingo shows about **2.1 million** learners of Polish from English on its
  courses page (a snapshot reported by [Duolingo](https://www.duolingo.com/courses);
  [Duoplanet](https://duoplanet.com/duolingo-languages-list/) gives 2.34 million
  in 2024). These are registered learners, not active ones.
- **r/learnpolish** has about **44,000** members, growing by about 5,000 a year
  ([GummySearch](https://gummysearch.com/r/learnpolish), third-party tracker,
  undated snapshot).
- A recurring complaint there: general apps (Duolingo, Babbel, Busuu) do not
  teach the cases well ([thread summary](https://lr.eu.psf.lt/r/learnpolish/comments/1fcksr8/how_to_learn_cases)).
  That is this app's opening.

### How to get real numbers (15 minutes, free)

1. **Google Keyword Planner** (free with a Google Ads account, no ad spend
   needed): paste the phrases from the table, set location to United States +
   United Kingdom + Ireland, language English. It gives ranges (10–100,
   100–1K, 1K–10K).
2. **Google Trends**: compare "polish cases", "polish grammar" and "polish verb
   conjugation" to see which is biggest relative to the others.
3. After launch, **Google Search Console** shows the real searches that brought
   people to the site. That beats any estimate; plan the topic sub-pages from it.

**What this implies**

- The top results for "polish cases" are a mix of one-topic sites and big
  general sites. **None of them ranks because of its name**: Preply, Vaia and
  Talkpal have no "Polish" in theirs, and they rank on authority and on a page
  titled for the query.
- Almost everything ranking is **reading material or static tests**. An
  interactive page ("explanation + table + try it now") is different, and
  Google's own guidance rewards pages that satisfy the intent. That is where the
  topic pages (§5) can win.
- The App Store niche is thin (apps with 0–5 ratings), so a well-titled app can
  rank there quickly, **but not under the name "Polish Cases"**, which is taken.

## 3. How much the name matters for SEO in 2026

| Question | Answer | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do keywords in the domain help Google rankings? | **No meaningful boost.** Google's John Mueller has said repeatedly that a keyword domain gives "no recognizable SEO advantage" and that top results often lack the keyword in the URL. Words in the extension (.app, .io) are ignored entirely. | [SEJ: Mueller on keyword domains](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/john-mueller-on-keyword-domain-names-and-branding-for-seo/504423/), [SEJ: keywords in domain name](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/keywords-in-domain-name/), [Search Engine Land: TLD keywords ignored](https://searchengineland.com/google-says-keywords-tld-part-url-ignored-ranking-purposes-251971) |
| What does Google recommend instead? | Pick a memorable brand name you can keep for years; the 2024 rewrite of Google's SEO Starter Guide moved from "keywords" to "branding" on this point. | [SEJ: Starter Guide, branding in, keywords out](https://searchenginejournal.com/googles-seo-starter-guide-updates-branding-in-keywords-out/506522), [SEJ: branding over keywords](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/googles-on-domain-name-selection-branding-over-keywords/492276/) |
| Are exact-match domains (e.g. polishcasespractice.com) penalised? | Not as such. Google's 2012 "EMD update" demoted **low-quality** sites on exact-match domains (0.6% of US queries), not keyword domains with good content. But a long keyword domain can look spammy to people, and it locks the product to one topic. | [Search Engine Roundtable on the EMD update](https://seroundtable.com/google-emd-update-15776.html), [Sistrix](https://www.sistrix.com/?p=32116), [Search Engine Land 2025](https://searchengineland.com/exact-match-domains-value-453837) |
| Where does the name show in results? | Google shows a **site name** above each result, taken from structured data (`WebSite` with `name` / `alternateName`), the title and the home page. A short, distinctive name is more likely to be shown as chosen. Google will not use the same site name for two global sites. | [Google: introducing site names](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2022/10/introducing-site-names-on-search?authuser=1), [Google: update on site names](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/07/sitenames) |
| Do brand searches help? | Indirectly. People searching the name (and "name + polish cases") show the site is a known destination, and a distinctive name is one only you can rank for. A generic name ("Polish Grammar") competes with every page about Polish grammar for its own brand search. | Commentary in the SEJ pieces above |
| App stores: does the name carry keywords? | **Yes, and here it does matter.** On iOS the 30-character app name is the strongest keyword field, then the 30-character subtitle, then the hidden 100-character keyword field. Google Play has one 30-character title plus an indexed short description. The store title does not have to equal the brand: "Polski Gym: Polish Grammar" is allowed. | [AppRadar: ranking factors 2026](https://appradar.com/academy/aso-basics/app-store-ranking-factors), [AppFollow: ASO title playbook 2026](https://appfollow.io/blog/app-store-optimization-title) (vendor guides; Apple and Google publish no weights) |
| AI answers (ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews) | They cite pages that answer the question clearly, and mention products by name. A distinctive name is easier for them to repeat correctly than a generic phrase. | Reasoned, not sourced |

**Bottom line for the owner:** the brand name is worth almost nothing for
Google rankings and something for the App Store title. The keywords go in page
titles, headings, slugs and the store title/subtitle (§5). So choose the name
for memorability, pronunciation, uniqueness and room to grow (the roadmap adds
more topics and interface languages later), with a light keyword hint if it
comes free.

### 4.3 Checking them yourself (one minute)

From any machine with normal internet access, `404` = not registered,
`200` = registered:

```bash
for n in polskigym odmiana polishgrammargym practicepolish polishcases polishgrammardrills sevencases; do
  printf "%-22s .com %s  .app %s  .io %s\n" "$n" \
    "$(curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://rdap.verisign.com/com/v1/domain/$n.com)" \
    "$(curl -sL -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://rdap.org/domain/$n.app)" \
    "$(curl -sL -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}' https://rdap.org/domain/$n.io)"
done
```

Or type the names into any registrar's search box (Cloudflare Registrar sells
at cost and the app already plans to deploy on Cloudflare Pages). Before
paying, also search the [EUIPO](https://euipo.europa.eu/eSearch/),
[USPTO](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/) and [WIPO Global Brand Database](https://branddb.wipo.int/)
registers for the chosen name in class 9 (software) and class 41 (education),
and search both app stores. This research found no conflicts for the
recommended names, but it is not a legal trademark search.

## 5. Recommendation

### Top pick: **Polski Gym**

- **Says what it is in four syllables.** "Polski" tells a learner it's Polish;
  "gym" says daily workouts, reps and streaks, which is exactly the SRS +
  "today's practice" loop. It doesn't promise only cases, so verbs, numbers and
  later topics fit.
- **Easy for English speakers.** No diacritics, spelt as it sounds, and learners
  already know "polski".
- **Keyword-friendly without being generic.** It contains a word learners
  search, and the store title "Polski Gym: Polish Grammar" (26 characters) puts
  "Polish grammar" in the strongest App Store field.
- **Ownable.** No app, site or course with this name turned up, so brand
  searches and the Google site name will be yours.
- Domains to try in order: **polskigym.com**, then **polskigym.app** (the
  `.app` extension forces HTTPS and suits a PWA), then getpolskigym.com.

### Runner-up: **Odmiana**

The pure brand option: a real Polish grammar word ("declension"), short,
distinctive, and memorable to anyone who has fought with the cases. It costs
more marketing (learners won't know the word at first, and the pronunciation
needs teaching), and **odmiana.com** is probably taken. Choose it if the owner
prefers a name that sounds like a product rather than a description; pair it
with a descriptive tagline everywhere ("Odmiana — Polish grammar practice").

If neither domain is obtainable at a sane price, **Polish Grammar Gym** is the
safe keyword-led fallback (same idea, more literal, longer).

### Title, H1 and description patterns (work with any name)

Google truncates titles at about 60 characters and descriptions at about 155,
so the keyword goes first and the brand last.

## 6. Colours and logo (for Polski Gym)

All pairs below were measured against WCAG 2.x: normal text needs 4.5:1, large
text and icons 3:1. "Light" is on white `#FFFFFF`, "dark" is on `#121212`.

| Direction | Feel | Accent, light theme | Accent, dark theme | Supporting | Contrast checks |

| C. Plum | Distinctive, a little premium (fits the later paid tier) | `#6B2E7A` | `#C79BD6` | Same neutrals | plum on white 9.14:1, white on plum 9.14:1, `#C79BD6` on dark 8.11:1 |

Right / near-miss / wrong feedback colours should stay separate from the brand
accent (keep the existing green / amber / red tokens), and never rely on colour
alone: keep the icons and text labels.

**Logo concept.** A dumbbell whose two weights are the letters **ę** and **ą**,
the two nasal vowels found in so many case endings (`kawę`, `z kawą`). In the
app icon: a white dumbbell on the accent colour, the
"weights" drawn as the ogonek tails (the little hooks under ą and ę), so it
reads as "gym" at 48 px and as "Polish" up close. Wordmark: **Polski Gym** in a
sturdy geometric sans (for example Space Grotesk or Inter Tight, both free),
"Polski" in the accent colour and "Gym" in ink. It works as one source SVG for
`scripts/icons.ts` (phase-3.md §1), monochrome for the maskable icon.
